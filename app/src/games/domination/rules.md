# Domination

Claim a number, then take over the board by spreading into neighbouring numbers. Hold enough of the board — or the most of it when time runs out — to win.

## Setup

Each player claims a **starting number**: throw a dart at any free number to take it (you can't start on the bull), or have numbers dealt at random. That number is your first territory, shown in your colour.

## Taking territory

On your turn (three darts) you can take over any number **next to one you already own**. "Next to" means neighbouring on the board — 20's neighbours are 5 and 1, not 19 and 21. The board lights up the numbers you can take.

- An **empty** number becomes yours with a single hit.
- A number an **opponent** owns takes **two** hits: one hit knocks them off it (it goes empty), a second makes it yours. A **double or treble does both at once**, taking it in one dart.
- Hitting a number that isn't next to your territory does nothing.

Because each number you take opens up its own neighbours, a good turn can march several numbers along the board.

## The bull

With **Bull** on, the bull is an extra territory connected to *every* number. Hold any number and you can always contest the bull; hold the bull and you can attack anywhere on the board. The same one/two-hit rule applies (a double-bull takes an enemy-held bull outright), and it counts toward the win.

## Winning

- **Domination to win** — reach that share of the board and you win at once (100% means taking the whole board).
- To knock a player **out** you must **take** their last number, not just neutralise it. Leave their last number merely empty at the end of your turn and it reverts to them, as if you never hit it — so finishing a player always costs a real capture: a double or treble, or two singles in one turn. The last player left in wins.
- If nobody reaches the target, whoever holds the **most territory** when the round limit is reached wins — a tie is a draw, or plays on to sudden death.

## Tips

Spreading wide grabs empty ground fast but leaves a long border to defend. A solid block is harder to break — an opponent needs two hits per number to punch through it.
